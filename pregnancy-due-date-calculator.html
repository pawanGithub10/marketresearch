How the Pregnancy Calculator Works

Naegele's Rule (Standard Method)

The most common method for estimating due date, recommended by ACOG (American College of Obstetricians and Gynecologists):

Due Date = First Day of LMP + 280 days

Or: First Day of LMP + 40 weeks + 0 days

This assumes a 28-day cycle and 14-day ovulation

Gestational Age Calculation

Medical professionals define pregnancy as 40 weeks from the first day of your last menstrual period:

Week 1: First day of last menstrual period (LMP)

Week 2: Ovulation and conception occur

Week 4: Positive pregnancy test possible

Week 8: Heartbeat detectable via ultrasound

Week 12: End of first trimester (12 weeks 6 days)

Week 20: Anatomy scan typically performed

Week 37: Full-term pregnancy (≥37 weeks)

Week 40: Due date / estimated date of delivery (EDD)

IVF & Assisted Reproduction Adjustments

For IVF pregnancies, add days based on embryo age at transfer:

3-Day Embryo:

Add 3 days to transfer date for 5-day equivalent

5-Day Blastocyst:

Most accurate dating method, use transfer date + 280 days

Delivery Window

Due date is an estimate. Full-term pregnancies are considered 37-42 weeks:

Early Term: 37 weeks 0 days to 38 weeks 6 days

Full Term: 39 weeks 0 days to 40 weeks 6 days

Late Term: 41 weeks 0 days to 41 weeks 6 days

Overdue (Post-term): 42 weeks 0 days and beyond

Excel & Google Sheets Formulas

Calculate Due Date from LMP

Add 280 days to the first day of last menstrual period:

=A1+280
(where A1 is your LMP date)
Or: =DATE(YEAR(A1), MONTH(A1), DAY(A1)+280)

Calculate Gestational Age in Weeks

=INT((TODAY()-A1)/7)&" weeks"
Or: =DATEDIF(A1,TODAY(),"W")&" weeks " & MOD(DATEDIF(A1,TODAY(),"D"),7)&" days"

Current Trimester

=IF(DATEDIF(A1,TODAY(),"W")<=13,"First","IF(DATEDIF(A1,TODAY(),"W")<=26,"Second","Third"))

Days Until Due Date

=(A1+280)-TODAY()
Where A1 is your LMP date

IVF Due Date Calculation

For 3-day embryo: =B1+282 (transfer date + 282 days)

For 5-day embryo: =B1+280 (transfer date + 280 days)

B1 = embryo transfer date

Python Implementation

from datetime import datetime, timedelta

def calculate_due_date(lmp_date):

"""Calculate due date from last menstrual period"""

if isinstance(lmp_date, str):

lmp_date = datetime.strptime(lmp_date, "%Y-%m-%d")

# Naegele's rule: LMP + 280 days

due_date = lmp_date + timedelta(days=280)

return due_date

def calculate_gestational_age(lmp_date):

"""Calculate current gestational age"""

if isinstance(lmp_date, str):

lmp_date = datetime.strptime(lmp_date, "%Y-%m-%d")

today = datetime.now()

days_since_lmp = (today - lmp_date).days

weeks = days_since_lmp // 7

days = days_since_lmp % 7

return {'weeks': weeks, 'days': days}

def get_trimester(weeks):

"""Determine current trimester"""

if weeks <= 13:

return "First Trimester"

elif weeks <= 26:

return "Second Trimester"

else:

return "Third Trimester"

def calculate_delivery_window(due_date):

"""Calculate full-term delivery window (37-42 weeks)"""

early_term = due_date - timedelta(days=21) # 37 weeks

late_term = due_date + timedelta(days=14) # 42 weeks

return {'early_term': early_term, 'late_term': late_term}

# Example usage:

lmp = "2024-04-15"

due = calculate_due_date(lmp)

age = calculate_gestational_age(lmp)

print(f"Due Date: {due.strftime('%B %d, %Y')}")

print(f"Gestational Age: {age['weeks']} weeks {age['days']} days")

print(f"Trimester: {get_trimester(age['weeks'])}")

How to Use the Pregnancy Calculator

Step 1: Choose Your Calculation Method

Select the method that matches your situation: "Last Period" (most common), "Conception" date, "Ultrasound" gestational age, or "IVF Transfer" date. Each method calculates your due date differently for maximum accuracy.

Step 2: Enter Your Date

For LMP: Use the first day of your last menstrual period. For conception: Use your best estimate if known. For ultrasound: Enter the date of your scan. For IVF: Enter your embryo transfer date.

Step 3: Select IVF Details (If Applicable)

If using IVF transfer dating, specify whether it was a 3-day embryo or 5-day blastocyst. 5-day blastocysts provide the most accurate dating. This adjusts your due date calculation accordingly.

Step 4: Review Your Due Date

Your estimated due date (EDD) is calculated. Remember this is an estimate—only about 4-5% of babies are born on their actual due date. Most babies arrive within 2 weeks before or after.

Step 5: Check Your Gestational Age

See how many weeks and days pregnant you are right now. This is calculated from your LMP, which is the medical standard. Your gestational age may differ from conception age by 1-2 weeks.

Step 6: Track Your Trimester

Identify which trimester you're in. First (weeks 1-13) focuses on organ development. Second (weeks 14-26) is typically easiest with weight gain. Third (weeks 27-40) involves final growth and preparation for delivery.

Step 7: Understand Delivery Window

Full-term pregnancy is 37-42 weeks. Doctors typically recommend delivery by 42 weeks. Your estimated delivery window shows when you can safely deliver. Babies born at 37+ weeks are considered full-term.

Step 8: Share With Healthcare Provider

Bring these results to your prenatal appointments. Your doctor may adjust your due date based on ultrasound measurements, especially if performed in the first trimester.

Frequently Asked Questions

How accurate is the due date calculator? ▼

The calculator is accurate within 3-5 days when using the first day of your last menstrual period. However, only 4-5% of babies are born on their exact due date. Most arrive within 2 weeks before or after. Ultrasound dating in the first trimester is accurate within 3-5 days; second trimester within 1-2 weeks; and third trimester within 2-3 weeks. This is why doctors may adjust your due date at different ultrasounds.

When should I use ultrasound dating vs LMP dating? ▼

LMP dating assumes a regular 28-day cycle with ovulation on day 14. If your cycles are irregular or you don't know your exact LMP, ultrasound dating is more accurate. First trimester ultrasounds (8-13 weeks) are the most precise for dating. If there's a discrepancy of more than 1-2 weeks between LMP and ultrasound dating, doctors typically trust the ultrasound result and adjust your due date accordingly.

What's the difference between due date and estimated date of delivery (EDD)? ▼

Due date and estimated date of delivery (EDD) are the same thing—they both refer to your baby's expected arrival date. Medical providers prefer "estimated date of delivery" because it emphasizes that this is an estimate, not a guarantee. Your actual delivery date will likely be within 2 weeks of this estimate.

What if I have irregular periods? ▼

If your periods are irregular (cycle length varies significantly), you should rely more on ultrasound dating than LMP dating. Use your best estimate for LMP in this calculator, but have an ultrasound for more accurate dating. First trimester ultrasounds are accurate within 3-5 days, so this is typically the most reliable method for irregular cycles.

Does irregular cycle length affect my due date? ▼

Yes. Naegele's rule assumes a 28-day cycle with ovulation on day 14. If your cycle is 35 days, you ovulate later (day 21), so conception occurs later. If your cycle is 21 days, ovulation is earlier (day 7). If you know your exact ovulation/conception date, use that instead of LMP for better accuracy. Otherwise, ultrasound dating provides the most reliable estimate.

What's the best dating method for IVF pregnancies? ▼

IVF embryo transfer dating is very accurate because the exact conception date is known. 5-day blastocyst transfers provide the most accurate dating—use transfer date + 280 days. For 3-day embryo transfers, add 282 days to the transfer date (to account for the 2-day development difference). IVF dating is typically accurate within 2-3 days.

Can I go past my due date? ▼

Yes. Pregnancies that continue beyond 42 weeks are considered overdue or post-term. Most doctors will induce labor by 42 weeks due to increased risks. However, small percentage of pregnancies naturally go 7-10 days past the due date. Medical providers monitor you carefully if you're overdue with extra ultrasounds and fetal monitoring (NSTs).

What trimester am I in? ▼

Pregnancy is divided into three trimesters: First trimester (weeks 1-13) covers organ formation; Second trimester (weeks 14-26) is often called the "golden period"; Third trimester (weeks 27-40+) involves rapid growth and preparation. Each has different concerns, symptoms, and screening tests. The calculator shows your current trimester based on your gestational age.

How the Mortgage Calculator Works

The Monthly Payment Formula

Your monthly mortgage payment is calculated using the standard amortization formula:

M = P × [r(1 + r)^n] / [(1 + r)^n − 1]

Where:

M = Monthly payment

P = Loan principal (home price - down payment)

r = Monthly interest rate (annual rate ÷ 12 ÷ 100)

n = Total number of payments (years × 12)

Example Calculation

For a $300,000 home with 20% down ($60,000), 6.5% rate, 30-year term:

Loan Amount: $300,000 - $60,000 = $240,000

Monthly Rate: 6.5% ÷ 12 ÷ 100 = 0.005417

Number of Payments: 30 × 12 = 360

Monthly Payment: $1,520 (principal + interest)

With taxes/insurance: $1,520 + $417 = $1,937

Affordability Guidelines

Lenders typically use these debt-to-income (DTI) ratios:

Front-end ratio: 28%

Housing costs ÷ gross monthly income

Back-end ratio: 36-43%

All monthly debt ÷ gross monthly income

Excel & Google Sheets Formulas

Monthly Payment Formula (PMT)

In Excel or Google Sheets, use the PMT function:

=PMT(rate/12, nper*12, -pv)
=PMT(6.5%/12, 30*12, -240000)
Result: $1,520.06

Total Interest Paid

=PMT(rate/12, nper*12, -pv) * nper * 12 - pv
Or simply: (PMT * number_of_payments) - principal

Remaining Balance After X Months

=PV(rate/12, (nper*12)-months_paid, -PMT)
Calculates remaining loan balance after specific months

Create Simple Amortization Table

Column A (Month): 1, 2, 3, ... 360

Column B (Payment): =PMT($rate/12, $nper*12, -$pv)

Column C (Interest): =A1*$rate/12 (starting balance × monthly rate)

Column D (Principal): =B1-C1

Column E (Balance): =E0-D1

Python Implementation

import math

def calculate_mortgage(home_price, down_payment_percent, annual_rate, years, annual_taxes_insurance=0):

"""Calculate mortgage payment and amortization"""

down_payment = home_price * (down_payment_percent / 100)

loan_amount = home_price - down_payment

monthly_rate = annual_rate / 12 / 100

num_payments = years * 12

# Calculate monthly payment using formula

if monthly_rate == 0:

monthly_payment = loan_amount / num_payments

else:

monthly_payment = loan_amount * (monthly_rate * (1 + monthly_rate)**num_payments) / ((1 + monthly_rate)**num_payments - 1)

monthly_tax_insurance = annual_taxes_insurance / 12

total_monthly = monthly_payment + monthly_tax_insurance

total_interest = (monthly_payment * num_payments) - loan_amount

total_paid = monthly_payment * num_payments

return {

'monthly_payment': round(monthly_payment, 2),

'total_monthly_with_taxes': round(total_monthly, 2),

'total_interest': round(total_interest, 2),

'total_paid': round(total_paid, 2),

'loan_amount': loan_amount,

'down_payment': down_payment

}

# Example usage:

result = calculate_mortgage(300000, 20, 6.5, 30, 5000)

print(f"Monthly Payment: ${result['monthly_payment']:,.2f}")

print(f"Total Interest: ${result['total_interest']:,.2f}")

How to Use the Mortgage Calculator

Step 1: Choose Your Calculation Type

Select "Purchase" if you're buying a new home and want to know how much your payment will be. Choose "Refinance" if you already have a mortgage and want to see savings from refinancing.

Step 2: Enter Home Price or Loan Amount

For purchases: Enter the full home price. For refinancing: Enter your current loan balance. Use the quick preset buttons for common amounts like $200K, $400K, or $600K.

Step 3: Set Your Down Payment

If buying, choose your down payment percentage (3-20%). Larger down payments reduce your monthly payment and may help you avoid PMI (Private Mortgage Insurance). Minimum is typically 3-5% for conventional loans.

Step 4: Enter Interest Rate

Current rates vary by credit score and loan type. Rates range from 3-8% depending on market conditions. You can either enter a specific rate or select your credit score range to see typical rates.

Step 5: Choose Loan Term

Most popular options are 15-year (faster payoff, higher payment) and 30-year (lower payment, more interest). 15-year typically has rates 0.3-0.5% lower than 30-year.

Step 6: Add Property Tax & Insurance

Enter your estimated annual property tax and homeowners insurance. Typically 0.8-1.5% of home value per year. Your lender will require insurance, and taxes vary by location.

Step 7: Review Your Results

Check your monthly payment, total interest, and affordability status. The calculator shows whether you can afford the home based on typical lending guidelines (your payment should be 28% or less of gross income).

Step 8: Experiment with Different Scenarios

Try adjusting the down payment, rate, or term to see how they affect your payment. Lower rates = lower payments. Longer terms = lower payments but more interest. Larger down payments = lower loan amount and payment.

Frequently Asked Questions

What's included in the monthly mortgage payment? ▼

Your monthly payment includes four components called "PITI": Principal (loan amount paid down), Interest (lender's cost), Taxes (property tax), and Insurance (homeowners insurance). PMI (Private Mortgage Insurance) may be added if your down payment is less than 20%.

How much of my payment goes toward principal vs interest? ▼

Early in the loan, most of your payment goes to interest. For example, on a 30-year mortgage, your first payment might be 85% interest and 15% principal. Over time, this reverses. After 20 years, you'll pay mostly principal. This is why making extra principal payments early saves significant interest.

What's the difference between 15-year and 30-year mortgages? ▼

A 15-year mortgage has higher monthly payments but you pay off the home faster and save significant interest. A 30-year mortgage has lower monthly payments but you pay more interest overall. For example, a $240K loan at 6.5%: 15-year payment is $1,851/month ($151k interest), 30-year payment is $1,520/month ($307k interest).

What credit score do I need for a mortgage? ▼

Most lenders require a minimum credit score of 620 for conventional loans, though scores below 680 have higher interest rates. FHA loans allow scores as low as 580 with a 3.5% down payment. VA loans (veterans) have no minimum score. Scores above 740 get the best rates.

What is PMI and can I avoid it? ▼

PMI (Private Mortgage Insurance) is required when your down payment is less than 20%. It costs 0.3-1.5% of the loan amount annually, typically $150-300/month on a $240K loan. You can avoid PMI by making a 20% down payment. PMI automatically cancels once you reach 20% equity through payments and home appreciation.

How much house can I afford based on my income? ▼

Lenders use the 28% rule: your housing payment shouldn't exceed 28% of your gross monthly income. For example, if you earn $5,000/month, your payment (including taxes/insurance) shouldn't exceed $1,400. Many use the 36% rule for total debt including student loans, car loans, credit cards, etc.

Should I refinance my mortgage? ▼

Refinancing makes sense if interest rates drop 0.5-1% below your current rate. Calculate the break-even point: (refinancing costs) ÷ (monthly savings) = number of months to break even. If you'll stay in the home longer than break-even, refinance. If rates are rising, refinance sooner rather than later.

How is the interest rate determined? ▼

Your rate depends on: (1) Federal Reserve rates (market baseline), (2) your credit score (better credit = lower rate), (3) loan amount and term, (4) down payment size, (5) your employment stability, and (6) current economic conditions. Shopping with multiple lenders can save you $50-200/month.

# How Much Mortgage Can You Afford? The Complete Affordability Guide

**Published:** November 2024 | **Reading Time:** 8 minutes | **Updated:** Monthly with current rates

## Executive Summary

Your mortgage shouldn't consume more than 28% of your gross monthly income according to industry standards. For a $75,000/year salary ($6,250/month gross), that means your maximum housing payment should be approximately **$1,750/month**. However, total debt obligations (including your mortgage) shouldn't exceed 36-43% of gross income. This guide breaks down exactly how much house you can actually afford.

---

## What is Mortgage Affordability?

Mortgage affordability refers to the relationship between your income and your housing costs. It's a mathematical assessment of whether you can comfortably pay your monthly mortgage, property taxes, insurance, and HOA fees without financial strain.

**The Key Principle:** Lenders use debt-to-income ratios (DTI) to determine how much you can borrow. Your DTI compares your monthly debt payments to your gross monthly income.

### Why Lenders Use DTI Ratios

Lenders discovered through decades of mortgage data that borrowers with DTI ratios above certain thresholds have significantly higher default rates. The ratios represent the maximum safe borrowing limit before financial hardship becomes likely.

**Real Example:** In the 2008 financial crisis, many homeowners had DTI ratios of 50%+ and couldn't afford their payments when unexpected expenses arose. Modern lending standards use conservative DTI limits to prevent this.

### The Two DTI Ratios

1. **Front-End DTI (Housing Ratio):** 28% maximum
   - Includes: mortgage payment, property tax, homeowners insurance, HOA fees
   - Formula: (Monthly mortgage payment ÷ Gross monthly income) × 100

2. **Back-End DTI (Total Debt Ratio):** 36-43% maximum
   - Includes: all housing costs PLUS car loans, student loans, credit cards, personal loans
   - Formula: (All monthly debt payments ÷ Gross monthly income) × 100

**Key Insight:** Most people run into trouble with the back-end DTI. You might qualify for a $500k mortgage, but if you have $1,000/month in student loans, that reduces your comfortable housing budget significantly.

## How to Calculate Your Affordable Mortgage Amount

### Step 1: Calculate Your Gross Monthly Income

Don't use your take-home pay—lenders calculate based on gross income (before taxes).

```
Annual salary: $75,000
Gross monthly income: $75,000 ÷ 12 = $6,250
```

### Step 2: Apply the 28% Front-End Rule

Calculate your maximum housing payment:

```
Maximum monthly housing payment = Gross income × 0.28
$6,250 × 0.28 = $1,750/month maximum
```

This $1,750 must cover:
- Principal & interest on mortgage
- Property tax
- Homeowners insurance
- HOA fees (if applicable)
- PMI (if down payment < 20%)

### Step 3: Estimate Your Housing Components

**Example for $300,000 home:**

| Component | Amount |
|-----------|--------|
| Home Price | $300,000 |
| Down Payment (20%) | $60,000 |
| Loan Amount | $240,000 |
| Interest Rate | 6.5% |
| Loan Term | 30 years |
| Monthly P&I | $1,520 |
| Property Tax (1.2%/yr) | $300 |
| Insurance (0.5%/yr) | $125 |
| HOA | $0 |
| **Total Monthly** | **$1,945** |

**Assessment:** At $6,250 gross income, this person shouldn't buy this home. ($1,945 > $1,750 maximum)

### Step 4: Back-Calculate Your Affordable Home Price

If your target payment is $1,750/month:

```
Available for P&I: $1,750 - $325 (taxes/insurance) = $1,425/month
Using mortgage formula: Loan amount ≈ $254,000
With 20% down: Home price ≈ $317,500
```

**This is your affordable price range:** $280,000-$320,000 (accounting for varying rates and tax rates)

### Step 5: Apply Back-End DTI Check

Calculate all monthly debt:

```
Mortgage payment: $1,425
Property tax/insurance: $325
Car loan: $400
Student loans: $250
Credit cards: $150
─────────────
Total debt: $2,550

DTI = $2,550 ÷ $6,250 = 40.8%
```

This person is at the maximum acceptable DTI. Any unexpected debt would make them over-extended. Most lenders would want to see 35-36% for comfort.

## Global Importance and Market Context

### Why Affordable Mortgages Matter

According to the [US Census Bureau Housing Data](https://www.census.gov/topics/housing.html), nearly 40% of homeowners spend more than 30% of income on housing costs. This creates several problems:

1. **Financial Fragility:** One job loss or medical emergency triggers default risk
2. **Economic Growth:** Over-extended homeowners can't spend on other goods/services, slowing economic growth
3. **Wealth Inequality:** Unaffordable housing locks people out of wealth building through home equity
4. **Mental Health:** Housing cost stress correlates with anxiety, depression, and family instability

### International Comparison

Different countries use different affordability standards:

- **United States:** 28% front-end / 36-43% back-end DTI
- **United Kingdom:** 4-4.5x gross annual income maximum ([Financial Conduct Authority](https://www.fca.org.uk))
- **Canada:** Similar to US (32% GDS, 40% TDS ratios)
- **Australia:** Typically 3-4x gross annual income

### Market Cycles and Affordability

Housing affordability varies dramatically by market cycle:

**Peak Unaffordability (2022):** In many US metros, the median home cost 7-8x median income
**Relative Affordability (2024):** Improved but still 5-6x median income in expensive markets

The [National Association of Realtors](https://www.nar.realtor/research-and-statistics) tracks affordability indices showing which markets are most stretched.

## Advanced Affordability Considerations

### The Hidden Costs Nobody Talks About

Your payment isn't just mortgage payment. Total housing costs include:

1. **Property Tax:** 0.4-2.5% of home value annually depending on state
   - [Zillow property tax guide](https://www.zillow.com) provides state averages
   - Can be $200-$500+ per month on affordable homes

2. **Homeowners Insurance:** $800-$2,000/year ($67-$167/month)
   - Varies by home value, location, claims history
   - [Insurance Information Institute](https://www.iii.org) has state data

3. **PMI (if <20% down):** 0.3-1.5% of loan annually
   - On a $250k loan with 10% down: $100-$300/month
   - [FHA loan calculator](https://www.hud.gov/program_offices/public_indian_housing) shows PMI examples

4. **HOA Fees:** $100-$500+ per month (if applicable)
   - Often forgotten in affordability calculations
   - Get actual HOA documents from seller

5. **Home Maintenance:** Expected $1-2% of home value annually
   - Budget $200-$400/month for 20-year reserves
   - [HomeAdvisor](https://www.homeadvisor.com) maintenance guides

6. **Utilities:** $150-$300/month typically
   - Varies by climate, efficiency, home size

**Total Additional Costs:** $1,200-$1,500/month beyond mortgage payment on a modest home

### Income Considerations

Lenders calculate "verifiable income":

1. **W-2 Employees:** Use last 2 years of tax returns
2. **Self-Employed:** Typically need 2 years average income (lower of last 2 years)
3. **Bonus/Commission:** Usually averaged over 2 years
4. **Rental Income:** 75% of verified rental income (expense accounts for vacancy)
5. **Investment Income:** Highly variable acceptance

### Down Payment Impact on Affordability

| Down Payment | Loan on $300k Home | PMI Cost | Monthly Impact |
|--------------|-------------------|----------|-----------------|
| 3% ($9,000) | $291,000 | $145/mo | Full impact |
| 5% ($15,000) | $285,000 | $110/mo | Lower impact |
| 10% ($30,000) | $270,000 | $80/mo | Further reduced |
| 20% ($60,000) | $240,000 | $0 | Eliminated |

**Key Insight:** Saving for 20% down increases your affordable price by $30,000-$50,000 by eliminating PMI.

### Credit Score Impact

Interest rates vary 1-3% based on credit score:

| Credit Score | Interest Rate | 30-Year Payment |
|--------------|---------------|-----------------|
| 580-619 | 7.5% | $1,678 |
| 620-679 | 6.8% | $1,605 |
| 680-739 | 6.2% | $1,534 |
| 740-799 | 5.8% | $1,498 |
| 800+ | 5.3% | $1,453 |

**On a $240,000 loan:** Score improvement from 620 to 740+ saves $180/month = $2,160/year

## The Affordability Mistake Most Buyers Make

Banks will pre-approve you for more than you should borrow. Getting approved for a $500,000 mortgage doesn't mean you should take it.

### Why Lenders Pre-Approve Too Much

1. Higher loan = higher lender profits
2. Pre-approval is based on mathematical DTI, not your actual lifestyle/expenses
3. Lenders don't account for your other financial goals (retirement, kids' college)

### The 20% Rule (More Conservative)

Many financial advisors recommend the 20% rule:

```
Affordable home price = Annual gross income × 2
```

For $75,000 income: Affordable price = $150,000 (much lower than DTI-based $317,500)

While stricter, this creates much greater financial security and flexibility.

Why EITC Matters

💰

Free Money You Earned

EITC is a refundable tax credit—money from IRS, not loans. If you earned it and have low-to-moderate income, you likely qualify.

📈

Bigger Refund with Kids

Max $6,728 with 3+ children. Can be largest refund many families receive. More than Child Tax Credit for low-income earners.

📊

You Must File to Get It

EITC is automatic only if filing return. Many low-income earners don't file but qualify. You must file to claim it.

How EITC Works

What: How EITC is Calculated

EITC has three phases based on income:

Phase 1: Credit Build-Up (0% to Income Limit)

Credit increases with each dollar earned. Rate: 34-45% depending on children.

Phase 2: Maximum Credit (Plateau)

Your income is within the flat maximum range—you get full credit amount.

Phase 3: Phase-Out (Income Limit to Upper Limit)

Credit decreases as income rises. Rate: 15-21% per $1k over limit.

Why: Purpose of EITC

EITC policy goals:

Reward work: Encourages employment vs. welfare dependency

Reduce poverty: Lifts millions of working families above poverty

Support families: Especially helps parents working low-wage jobs

Stimulate economy: Money returned to low-income workers gets spent locally

How: Phase-Out Example

Example: Single filer, 1 child, 2024

Max EITC: $3,733

Income limit: $46,560

Phase-out rate: 21%

If your income is $50,000:

Over limit: $50,000 - $46,560 = $3,440

Phase-out: $3,440 × 21% = $723 reduction

Your EITC: $3,733 - $723 = $3,010

Global Context: Earned Income Credits Worldwide

US has most generous EITC globally:

United States: Max $6,728 per year (refundable tax credit)

UK: Working Family Tax Credit (similar, smaller amounts)

Canada: Canada Workers Benefit (CWTB), similar structure

Australia: Tax offsets and family payments (different structure)

Impact: EITC lifts ~6 million people out of poverty annually in US

Key EITC Requirements

✓ Have earned income

Wages (W-2) or self-employment (Schedule C). Investment income doesn't count.

✓ Income below limit

Your income and AGI must be under the limit for your filing status/children.

✓ Valid SSN or ITIN

You and spouse (if MFJ) must have Social Security Number or valid ITIN.

✓ Qualifying children (if claiming)

Must be your child, under 17, have valid SSN, and live with you 6+ months.

✓ File a tax return

EITC is not automatic—you must file Form 1040 to claim it.

✓ Be US citizen/resident

Permanent resident aliens qualify; most others must be citizens.

How to Claim Your EITC

Step 1: Determine if You Qualify

Use our calculator above. Check: (1) You have earned income, (2) Your income is below limit, (3) You have valid SSN, (4) Children meet requirements if claiming them.

Step 2: Gather Your Documents

Collect: W-2 forms (or Schedule C if self-employed), children's Social Security numbers, proof of residency, Social Security card or ITIN.

Step 3: File Your Tax Return

File Form 1040 with Schedule EIC (EITC form). File with IRS (free with VITA, or paid tax software). Free options at IRS.gov for low-income filers.

Step 4: Receive Your Refund

If eligible, IRS deposits EITC into your bank account (direct deposit is fastest). Or receive check by mail in 4-6 weeks. Can claim on tax return OR request advance EITC from employer (rare).

Where to File

Free options: IRS VITA program (free in-person help), IRS Free File (online), tax preparation nonprofits. Paid options: Tax software ($0-150), tax preparer ($100-500).

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I have to file a tax return to get EITC? ▼

Yes. EITC is not automatic—the IRS doesn't know you're eligible unless you file. Many low-income workers don't file because they don't owe taxes, but filing is required to claim EITC. This is money you earned and qualify for; filing is worth it.

What if I have self-employment income? ▼

Self-employment counts as earned income for EITC. Use net profit from Schedule C. You must file Schedule C and Form 1040 to claim EITC. Self-employed people often qualify for EITC because net business income is usually lower than gross revenue.

Can I claim children who don't live with me full-time? ▼

Child must live with you more than half the year (6+ months). Overnight visits during summer don't count unless it's your primary custody arrangement. The child must have valid SSN and be claimed as your dependent on tax return.

Is EITC really free money? ▼

Yes. EITC is a refundable tax credit—meaning if it's larger than your tax bill, IRS sends you the difference. It's not a loan. You don't have to repay it. Congress designed it as benefit for working low-income people. Use it if you qualify.

What if I made a mistake on prior years? ▼

You can amend prior years with Form 1040-X (Amended Return). Go back 3 years typically. If you qualified but didn't file, you might be able to claim past years' EITC. Use IRS VITA for help or consult tax professional. Better to fix it than ignore it.

How is EITC different from Child Tax Credit? ▼

Child Tax Credit (CTC): $2,000 per child under 17; doesn't require earned income; partially refundable. EITC: Requires earned income; up to $6,728 with 3+ kids; fully refundable. Both are separate—you can claim both if eligible. EITC better for low-income earners; CTC better for moderate/higher income.

What's the deadline to claim EITC? ▼

Tax return deadline is April 15 (typically). File by then to claim EITC for that year. If filing late, you can claim EITC with amended return (Form 1040-X) up to 3 years later. Don't miss claiming it just because you file late—amend and get your refund.
